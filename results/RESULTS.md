## Reading these numbers honestly

- The test programs are small and were written for this project; they show the detector works as designed, they are not a sample of real-world bugs.
- ThreadSanitizer is mainly a data-race detector (its deadlock detector is a side feature) and needs a recompile. Helgrind needs no recompile but runs the program on a simulated CPU.
- **ThreadSanitizer was faster than LockSpy on: `perf/uncontended_loop 1000000`.** LockSpy serialises every lock event of every thread on one private lock, so a tight lock/unlock loop in several threads pays for that; TSan keeps per-thread state.
- Most correct/ programs run for a few milliseconds, so their slowdown factors are dominated by start-up cost (loading the library, starting the watchdog) rather than per-lock cost.
- For the programs that really hang (`--hang`, `philosophers_naive`, `recursive_lock`), ThreadSanitizer and Helgrind were killed by the timeout; only what they printed before that counts. LockSpy's watchdog reported and aborted.
- ThreadSanitizer was run under `setarch -R` (ASLR off), which GCC 13's TSan needs on this kernel.
- LockSpy's selling points are that it needs no recompile, separates *potential* from *live* deadlocks, filters gate-lock / single-thread / trylock patterns, and prints a concrete lock order as the fix.
