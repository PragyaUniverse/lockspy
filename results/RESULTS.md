## Precision and recall (program level)

Positives: the buggy programs. Negatives: the correct and tricky programs. precision = caught / (caught + false alarms), recall = caught / buggy programs.

| tool | caught | missed | false alarms | clean | precision | recall | exact finding types* | recompile needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LockSpy | 9 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 16/16 | no |
| LockSpy --naive | 9 | 0 | 3 | 4 | 0.75 | 1.00 | 13/16 | no |
| ThreadSanitizer | 6 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 0.75 | 0.67 | 10/16 | yes |
| Helgrind | 8 | 1 | 3 | 4 | 0.73 | 0.89 | 10/16 | no |

\* exact = the set of finding types equals the expected set (LockSpy's own vocabulary; other tools do not report live deadlocks or held-at-exit the same way, so this column favours LockSpy by design).

- LockSpy: geometric-mean slowdown **3.1x** over these 6 programs.
- ThreadSanitizer: geometric-mean slowdown **4.3x** over these 6 programs.
- Helgrind: geometric-mean slowdown **122.5x** over these 6 programs.
- Uncontended lock+unlock pair: about **185 ns** extra per pair under LockSpy (4000000 pairs, 4 threads, each on its own mutex).

## Notes

- These are small programs we wrote for this project, not real-world bugs.
- ThreadSanitizer is mostly a data-race detector and needs a recompile. Helgrind doesn't, but it runs the program on a simulated CPU, which is why it's so slow.
- ThreadSanitizer was faster than LockSpy on `perf/uncontended_loop 1000000`. All our bookkeeping goes through one internal lock, so a tight lock/unlock loop in several threads suffers; TSan keeps its state per thread.
- Most of the correct/ programs finish in a few milliseconds, so their slowdown is mostly start-up time.
- The programs that really hang (`--hang`, `philosophers_naive`, `recursive_lock`) were killed by the timeout under ThreadSanitizer and Helgrind; we counted whatever they printed before that.
- ThreadSanitizer was run with `setarch -R` (ASLR off). GCC 13's TSan crashes on this kernel otherwise.
